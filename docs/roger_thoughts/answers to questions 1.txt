Question Q1: Is a Work a first-class thing?
Answer: Initially, we do not need to expose the higher-level "work" concept through the user interface.  Building the "work" concept requires more effort for "productions" to be created, i.e. to be accurately tied to a higher-level "work" object.  However, ultimately it will be nice to be able to search for all productions of a certain work, and see the ratings of them, so it makes sense to introduce the "work" object and its relationships internally.

Question Q2: Where is the boundary of one production?
Your answer is correct: these entities are all separate productions: Hamilton on Broadway, Hamilton in the West End, the Hamilton US national tour, and a 2026 revival of a show that closed in 2010.  A "revival" is by definition a new production.

And yes, a production that opens off-broadway but transfers to Broadway with the same team (producers, cast, director, etc.) is still the same production, it's just that its run continued at a different location.  Yes, let's define "engagement" as "a production's stay at one venue over a date range".

Note that it is optional for a "production" to specify its engagements on our site, though we want to support as much detail as they will provide us.  A touring production would, for example, upload their entire itinerary (list of engagements, with locations and dates).  But minimally we could define a production with only a  "unique production name" (since all productions will need a unique defining name separate from the Work), a starting year, and an optional text-paragraph description of the production.
